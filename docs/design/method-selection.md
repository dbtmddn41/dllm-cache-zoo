# Cache method selection

**14 November 2026.** Start on `GSAI-ML/LLaDA-8B-Instruct` with Fast-dLLM v1 Prefix, then Dual, dLLM-Cache and dKV-Cache Decode. Full recomputation is the control. The next three entries are the exact completed-block cache on SDAR, d²Cache and Elastic-Cache. These seven settings fill the first two milestones and come from six main-conference papers. Four more are selected to follow them: dKV-Cache Greedy, Fast-dLLM v2 Sub-block, DPad and Sparse-dLLM. That makes eleven settings from nine papers. Every other candidate waits, and its entry below names what would bring it back. [Roadmap](roadmap.md) · [Upstream examples by checkpoint](../methods.md)

**Status on 15 November 2026.** All eleven settings run from the Zoo and were checked against their references. The work estimates in the tables below were written before the implementation and are kept as the record of the decision. [Check report](../checks.md)

When filling the second of three response blocks, Prefix recomputes blocks two and three. Dual also keeps the masked third block's keys and values, so only block two goes through the model again until the next block boundary. Both approximate the full bidirectional model. A block-causal checkpoint can reuse a completed block exactly because later blocks cannot change it. This is why the SDAR baseline gets a separate entry. [Fast-dLLM generation code](https://github.com/NVlabs/Fast-dLLM/blob/a9b81e4caa240c8cad4f7dc1889ff4852a0fca5b/v1/llada/generate.py#L132-L272) · [Block Diffusion paper](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/hash/7ede97c3e082c6df10a8d6103a2eebd2-Abstract-Conference.html)

## How the decision was made

We used main-track proceedings or the conference program to establish acceptance. Findings papers, workshop papers and papers under review were not counted. For adoption we gave most weight to a selectable implementation in another project, followed by an independent team's reported use as an experimental baseline. Stars are GitHub repository totals observed on the survey date. They measure interest, can span multiple methods, and cannot establish production usage. Citation counts are Semantic Scholar totals on the same date, and they include comparisons that never ran the code. The effort estimates come from reading source paths against the planned host-owned generation loop. Zoo runtime compatibility and timings require testing.
